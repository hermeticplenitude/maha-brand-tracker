Brand tracking project · Edition 1 · Data as of Oct 2, 2026

Two questions, kept apart: how much attention does MAHA attract, and how many people actually identify with it?

Search data measures attention. Polls measure identification. News and social research measure how the brand is being talked about. This tracker holds all three side by side, with every number traced to its source and every ambiguity flagged.

                    Method, refresh and sources

                    How the data is collected

                    Attention. Google Trends has no public API. Series are pulled from Google Trends' own explore and widget endpoints inside a browser session and saved as JSON. Settings are held constant: United States (or Worldwide where stated), All categories, Web search. Google Topic entities (/g/11x8sdrsf0 MAHA, /g/11bw1_6lwn MAGA, /m/02l5km RFK Jr.) are used wherever ambiguity matters; the MAHA entity only has data from late April 2025.

                    Identification. Every national poll found that asks about MAHA directly is included with its construct (favorable / support / identify / part of movement), field dates, population, n and margin of error. Derived figures (such as MAHA-not-MAGA) are labelled as derived and never shown as a pollster's own number.

                    News. Two layers. The live layer reads Google News RSS search feeds (US edition) for eight MAHA queries over the trailing 7 days, merges and deduplicates them, and labels each headline for tone, origin of criticism and theme with a language model under a fixed rubric; only the headline and outlet are read, never the article. The Edition 1 layer is a hand-coded sample of 31 headlines kept as a benchmark. Neither is a census of coverage; GDELT volume on the Social panel is the closest thing to one.

                    Social and news volume. X counts come from the X API's counts endpoint: the platform's own daily total of original posts (retweets excluded) matching the phrase set, so they are a census rather than a sample; a capped 7-day sample of posts supplies the top-post and top-account lists. Bluesky posts are gathered from the network's public search endpoint for the phrase set (“Make America Healthy Again”, MAHA + Kennedy/RFK/movement/moms), deduplicated, and filtered so bare-acronym posts need a political cue. News volume is GDELT's share of all monitored US-source articles mentioning the full phrase, by day, averaged to weeks since August 2024. Both are machine counts, not hand-coded; Bluesky skews left of the electorate and is read as the critical-outside channel. Collectors are in the repository.

                    Refresh cadence

                    Edition 1 is a point-in-time snapshot. Each data file carries its own updated date, shown in the status row at the top. A weekly refresh re-runs the Trends pulls, adds any new poll releases, and re-codes the news sample.
